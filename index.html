In plain words

About 1 in 3 adults sleeps less than 7 hours, and these people feel more tired during the day. When short sleepers sleep longer, their fatigue goes down and their energy goes up. People who already sleep 7–9 hours should not expect much more energy from extra sleep.

A · How many people sleep too little?

NHANES, USA 2005–08 · % of adults by usual sleep, n = 10,896

More than 1 in 3 US adults sleep under 7 hours.37.1% sleep < 7 h; 60.5% sleep 7–9 h (Wheaton et al., 2011).

B · Are short sleepers more tired?

NHANES · % who have trouble concentrating because they feel tired

Yes. 29 in 100 short sleepers vs 19 in 100 normal sleepers.All six daily-life problems were more common with < 7 h (Wheaton et al., 2011).

C · Does losing sleep lower energy?

PubMed meta-analysis of experiments · effect size (Hedges’ g)

Yes. Sleep loss causes a large drop in positive mood and vigour.g = −0.94 for positive mood vs +0.45 for negative mood (Tomaso et al., 2021).

Conclusion

GRADE-inspired rating (Guyatt et al., 2008)

Losing sleep lowers energy and positive mood — 154 experiments (Palmer et al., 2024)High
Short sleepers who sleep longer feel less fatigued (Kamdar et al., 2004; Mah et al., 2011)Moderate
Little extra energy for people already sleeping 7–9 h (Mantua et al., 2019)Low / none
≥ 9 h linked to higher mortality, HR 1.29 (Gu et al., 2024)Caution

Track sleep first, aim for 7–9 hours, let the data decide.
